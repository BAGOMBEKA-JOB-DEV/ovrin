# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Assume good faith. Disagree about the work, not about the person.

## Scope

This applies in the issue tracker, pull requests, discussions, commit messages
and any other space this project occupies, and to anyone taking part.

## What is expected

- Argue about code, decisions and evidence. Ovrin's documentation is full of
  stated trade-offs precisely so they can be argued with.
- Say when you do not know something. Nobody here knows everything about PDF.
- Assume the person you are replying to is competent and busy.
- Accept that a maintainer may decline a contribution for reasons of scope or
  maintenance burden rather than quality. It is not a judgement of you.
- Give feedback about the change, and be specific enough to act on.

## What is not

- Personal attacks, insults, or comments about someone's identity,
  nationality, employer or level of experience.
- Harassment of any kind, in public or in private.
- Deliberately wasting people's time — bad-faith arguments, repeatedly
  relitigating settled decisions without new evidence, or demanding
  unpaid labour.
- Publishing anyone's private information.
- Submitting a document to the evaluation corpus that you do not have the right
  to share, or that contains someone's real personal data. This is a serious
  breach, not a paperwork error.

## Reporting

Email **bagombekajob@gmail.com**. Reports are read by the maintainer only and
are handled confidentially.

Ovrin has one maintainer, which means there is no committee and no appeals
process. It also means a response may not be immediate. Responses range from a
private word to a permanent ban, and the maintainer's decision is final.

If your report concerns the maintainer, and you would rather not send it to
them, you have no good option within this project. That is an honest limitation
of a single-maintainer project rather than a policy, and it is stated so that
nobody discovers it at the worst moment.
